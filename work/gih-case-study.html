The Problem

One small team.
An enormous operation.

Endeavour Group's Gaming Product Team was responsible for the performance of every gaming machine across every venue they operated. That meant constant decisions about machine selection, game content, jackpot settings, cabinet changes, and venue mix.

The team was highly experienced. The problem was not expertise. The problem was that the scale of decisions had grown beyond what any team could comfortably manage through manual analysis and spreadsheets.

Each decision required cross-referencing venue demographics, machine performance data, player behaviour patterns, regulatory constraints, and seasonal trends. Simultaneously. Across hundreds of venues.

The organisation didn't need better optimisation models. It needed a scalable way to make expert decisions, faster, across a complexity that was already beyond manual control.

Insight 01
The Expertise Bottleneck
Decision quality depended entirely on specialist knowledge held by a small group. There was no mechanism to scale that knowledge across the operation.
Insight 02
Data Was Not the Problem
The organisation had data. The real friction was the effort required to review it, interpret it, and convert it into confident operational action at scale.
Insight 03
Workflow Matters as Much as Logic
Recommendations without a surrounding workflow to support review, actioning, and feedback would not change behaviour. The whole loop needed designing.
Insight 04
Trust Has to Be Earned
Stakeholders had decades of experience with spreadsheets and manual methods. AI adoption required transparency, rationale visibility, and iterative trust-building.

The Defining Moment

The project we were hired for
was not the project we needed to solve.

Original Brief

→
Reframed Challenge

How might experts make faster, more scalable, more confident decisions across a complex multi-venue ecosystem?

Product Vision Expanded
From isolated optimisation models to a centralised decision intelligence platform. This shift unlocked the case for building a dedicated product rather than extending an existing reporting tool.
Research Direction Redirected
Instead of studying machine mathematics, discovery focused on how decisions were actually made, where friction lived, and what made stakeholders trust or distrust a recommendation.
Design Philosophy Grounded
The pivot made one thing clear: the system's job was not to automate decisions, but to make human experts faster, more confident, and more consistent at making them.

Design Strategy

Five principles that
shaped every decision

01
Human in Control
AI proposes. Experts decide. The system never removes agency. Every recommendation surface maintains clear override capability and decision ownership for the analyst.
02
Explainable Intelligence
Stakeholders needed to ask "why this recommendation?" and get a credible answer. Trust cannot be assumed. Every recommendation includes rationale, confidence signals, and supporting evidence.
03
Speed with Clarity
Reducing review time was the primary operational goal. But not at the cost of understanding. Design work centred on surfacing the right information at the right moment, not displaying everything.
04
Transparency and Feedback
AI systems improve through learning loops. The platform captures accepted and rejected recommendations, plus rejection reasoning, creating a feedback channel from human expertise back into the model.
05
Designed for Scale
Most tools were built for single-venue casino operations. Endeavour needed enterprise-scale portfolio visibility alongside venue-level operational detail. Both had to coexist in the same product.
What Was Built

Four capabilities that changed how the team worked

01
Core Workflow

Recommendation Console

The centre of the platform. Designed not as a feed to read, but as a decision environment to act from: review, analyse, question, and action in one place.

Explainability layer — "why this recommendation?" rationale expanded view
The Problem
The team spent days cross-referencing spreadsheets, multiple data sources, and manual notes to evaluate each recommendation. The process was slow, inconsistent, and difficult to delegate.
The Design Decision
Recommendations were designed as structured decision objects, each containing the change, expected impact, rationale, confidence level, and supporting evidence. The console became the primary workspace for review, not a passive report.
The Outcome
Review cycles that previously required multiple days could routinely be completed in approximately half a day. Acceptance rate reached 95%, reflecting stakeholder trust in recommendation quality and transparency.
02
Spatial Intelligence

Venue Map System

Gaming decisions are inherently spatial. A machine's position on the floor matters. This feature transformed static floor plans into a live analytical layer.

The Problem
Venue maps existed only as static image files. Analysts had no way to overlay performance data spatially, meaning they could see the numbers but not the patterns on the floor.
The Design Decision
Venue layouts were converted to SVG-based interactive assets, enabling dynamic data overlays at the machine level. A naming-convention strategy was introduced so new venues could be onboarded without requiring code changes.
The Strategic Value
Beyond usability, this reinforced a core product philosophy: visual systems accelerate understanding in complex enterprise environments. Spatial intelligence became a meaningful differentiator from every competing tool in the market.
03
Learning Loop

Feedback Analyser

Recommendations are only as good as what you learn from them. This module closed the loop between human decisions and machine learning, making rejection as valuable as acceptance.

Feedback Analyser Dashboard
Acceptance & rejection patterns
Feedback Analyser — recommendation outcome patterns and rejection reasoning
The Problem
When analysts declined a recommendation, the reasoning disappeared. There was no mechanism to understand why certain recommendations were repeatedly rejected, or to feed that understanding back into the model.
The Design Decision
The Feedback Analyser surfaced accepted and rejected recommendations alongside rejection reasoning and behavioural patterns. This created a human expertise to machine intelligence bridge that grew more valuable over time.
Why This Matters
In AI-enabled systems, what users reject is often more informative than what they accept. Capturing this signal systematically was central to the platform's long-term value proposition and to earning sustained stakeholder trust.
04
Operational Workflow

Order Planning & Analysis

Decisions do not end at the recommendation. They become physical machine orders, installation schedules, and manufacturer coordination. This feature brought that downstream reality into the platform.

The Discovery
The team used approximately ten different order sheet formats, with similar information appearing under different naming conventions across documents. Fragmentation created inconsistency and coordination overhead.
The Design Contribution
Order workflow analysis led to standardised column definitions, improved export structures, and a single operational environment supporting the entire order lifecycle. This illustrates how design contribution extended well beyond interface work.
The Broader Point
Enterprise UX is often workflow design in disguise. The most impactful work on this project did not happen in screen layouts. It happened in understanding the full operational system around the screen.

Reflection

What this project taught me about design leadership

Learning 01

Designing for AI is designing for trust

Recommendation quality alone does not drive adoption. Users need transparency, rationale, and confidence signals before they will change behaviour based on a system's output. Trust is a design problem. It has to be deliberately engineered into every surface that touches a recommendation.

Learning 02

Enterprise UX is often workflow design

The most impactful contributions on this project were not screen-level decisions. They were decisions about information flow, operational sequencing, and how expertise moves through an organisation. If you are not thinking about the system around the interface, you are thinking too small.

Learning 03

Product definition is sometimes a design responsibility

In environments where roadmap clarity is incomplete and stakeholder responsiveness varies, design leadership means stepping into the product definition gap. This is not overstepping. It is what keeps work moving and keeps the team aligned when prescription is not available.

Learning 04

Ambiguity can become a design strength

This project contained more ambiguity than most. Scope changed. Priorities shifted. Data constraints arrived mid-project. The design response was to treat ambiguity as material: use it to create structure, direction, and alignment where none existed. That capability is undervalued and rarely articulated.
